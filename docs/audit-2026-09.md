# Pre-review audit — September 2026

A last pass over the site before it goes to the client for review. The
brief: keep it professional, make it read a notch more premium, leave the
practice-page copy exactly as written.

## What "premium" means right now

The reference points were the current design conversation on YouTube,
pulled through Eden and read in full rather than skimmed:

- **Sam Crawford, "The Psychology of Premium Websites"** (210K views). Three
  principles. The *halo effect*: a visitor forms a judgment in about 50 ms
  and it colours everything after, so the hero is the whole first
  impression — engineer one feeling. *Cognitive fluency*: generous space,
  one goal per section, a clear hierarchy; "we are so confident we don't
  need to shout." *Micro-interactions and the peak–end rule*: cheap sites
  are static; premium sites respond to you, and people remember the peaks
  and how it ended.
- **Flux Academy, "How To Fix A Boring Template"** and **"Simple Websites
  Can Be The Best."** Typography is step one — the right face, confident
  sizes, mixed hierarchy — and "some great designers just stop there."
  Details on top of a plain layout (a rule between elements, a *scroll*
  cue, text aligned to a line) are what stop it reading as a template.
  A Swiss grid, good proportions and good photography can carry a site
  with no effects at all.
- **Flux Academy, "How Some Web Designers Charge 100x More."** Proof
  reduces risk: named people, credentials, a clear "what happens next."
- **Nate Herk** and **Jack Roberts** (Fable 5.1 / Claude Design breakdowns).
  The tells of a generated site: three feature cards, frosted glass, an
  indigo gradient, a generic headline. Feedback as you scroll (text that
  settles in) reads as premium; a nav that stays with you reads as
  considered. "One screen, one thought; one ask per screen."

## Where the site stood

Strong on the things that are hardest to add later: the type system, the
palette discipline, the photography, and fidelity to the approved deck.
The gaps were all in the last two principles.

1. No call to action anywhere on the site except the nav.
2. The homepage ended on two "— Watson" lines, which read as a bug (both
   attorneys share the surname) and gave the page no ending.
3. The practice ledger left the right half of a desktop screen empty.
4. Interactions were instant (underlines snapped in) rather than crafted.
5. The footer was a single line: no phone numbers, no site map.
6. Section spacing was fixed at 5 rem regardless of viewport.

## What changed

All within the brand rules — oxblood remains hairlines, labels, numerals
and link states; the motion vocabulary is still rules drawing themselves,
ink receding and returning, content unfolding along rules.

**Homepage.** Hero taller (82/86 vh), a paper hairline that draws itself
above the kicker, a *Scroll* cue at the foot. A proof strip beneath it —
400+ appeals · Two attorneys · Two offices — every line derived from copy
already approved elsewhere on the site. The ledger becomes two-column
editorial rows above 768 px with a *Read →* cue on hover. The attorneys
section gains a head, full names, and links to the biographies. The page
now closes on a Contact block (the approved site description, the offices,
and *Discuss a matter →*).

**Practice pages.** The blurb's opening paragraph is set a step larger, as
the approved mockup does; the words are untouched. A single *Discuss a
matter →* link sits below the blurb on a hairline.

**Global.** Nav underlines draw in and withdraw. The interior header is
sticky. Below-the-fold blocks settle in on scroll with a CSS-only
scroll-driven animation (no JavaScript, nothing gated, reduced motion
respected). Section air is fluid (5–7.5 rem). Photo headers and the
courthouse band are taller. The footer is a three-column colophon:
wordmark and tagline, offices with tel: links and the mailing address, and
a site map.

## For the client's attention

Nothing new was written for the firm's voice; every sentence on the site
is still theirs or the deck's. Three things are new *arrangements* of
approved text and should get a glance:

- The proof strip: "400+" as a numeral (the deck says "more than four
  hundred"), and each attorney's first credential set side by side.
- The closing block on the homepage reuses the site description from
  `config/site.ts`.
- "Discuss a matter" as the link label on the homepage and practice pages.
